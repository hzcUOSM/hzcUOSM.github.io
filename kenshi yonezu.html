Breaking Records
By Skream Originally published in Skream!

Kenshi Yonezu's music video for 'Lemon (Lemon)' surpassed 1 billion YouTube views at 11:08 PM on November 3rd. This milestone was achieved 8 years and 249 days after its release on February 27, 2018. It marks the first time a music video for a song with Japanese lyrics has reached 1 billion views, setting a new benchmark in Japanese music history.


'Lemon (Lemon)', written as the theme song for the TBS Friday drama 'アンナチュラル (Unnatural)', is a poignant song about the sadness and loss of losing a loved one. It resonated deeply with the world of the original drama, starring 石原さとみ, written by 野木亜紀子, and directed by 塚原あゆ子; Kenshi Yonezu's own grandfather's passing greatly influenced the song during its creation.

'Lemon (Lemon)' has quietly but steadily continued to spread, connecting with each individual's precious memories. Finally, it has reached an unprecedented 1 billion views for a Japanese song. To commemorate this achievement, comments have arrived from Kenshi Yonezu himself, as well as 'アンナチュラル (Unnatural)' star 石原さとみ, scriptwriter 野木亜紀子, and director 塚原あゆ子.


Furthermore, today, November 4th, at 8 PM, live footage of 'Lemon (Lemon)' from Kenshi Yonezu's 2025 TOUR / JUNK, held at Tokyo Dome in 2025, will be premiered on Kenshi Yonezu's official YouTube channel.


Comment from Kenshi Yonezu:

To be honest, I still don't quite grasp why 'Lemon (Lemon)' became such a widely loved and significant song. Of course, I never intended to create a trivial song, and I also understand that this song is the theme song for 'アンナチュラル (Unnatural)', an unequivocally great drama, and that it became a phenomenon because the drama production team treated it with love and care. However, soon after 'Lemon (Lemon)' was released, witnessing its reputation spread at an incredible pace, I distinctly remember being dumbfounded, thinking, 'Oh, so this song is going to be my representative song?' It was like we lit a small fire with scraps, expecting a campfire, but it quickly spread, eventually escalating into a forest fire. With a lighter in my right hand and a bucket of water in my left, that's roughly how the scene looked to me, the person directly involved.

As a child, I often saw great musicians express indifference towards their signature songs. Every time they showed a sulky attitude, saying things like 'I don't know what's good about this song' or 'I don't like it, so I don't want to play it live,' I used to inwardly curse, 'Anyone can hear it's a great song, don't play coy.' But now that it's happened to me, I finally understand their attitude. In other words, these kinds of songs intensely outpace the creator's intentions and feelings, rapidly becoming almost public domain. I suppose I can now sympathize with their sulkiness to the extent that 'Lemon (Lemon)' has allowed me to take a seat at the edge of the table where those great musicians gather.

Though this may have inadvertently turned into something of a grudge, I have consistently felt immense gratitude for 'Lemon (Lemon)' since that time. It gave me great bewilderment as a wild, rustic stray dog, showed me unknown landscapes, and led me to a life that is amusingly vexing. There are nights when I envision a future where I become a grumpy old man, strumming 'Lemon (Lemon)' in front of youngsters four generations younger, and being roughly treated with comments like 'Stop that dreary song' or 'Nobody remembers that.' But well, that's fine too.
